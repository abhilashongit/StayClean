Evidence

Built on ideas that research already supports.

Five findings behind the design: deciding ahead, binding yourself, protecting sleep, pacing social media, and eating earlier. Each one links to its source.

Planning ahead
d = 0.65

If-then plans beat good intentions.

A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests found that deciding in advance what you will do in a given situation had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals.

In Stay CleanYour 12–5 am rule is a plan made in daylight, enforced at night.
Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38. Source ↗
Binding yourself
+3 pts

Commitment still worked a year later.

In a randomized trial of about 2,000 smokers, those offered a voluntary commitment contract were around 3 percentage points more likely to pass a 6-month test, and the effect held in surprise tests at 12 months. Only 11% took it up.

In Stay CleanThe Device Admin lock makes undoing it a deliberate step, not an impulse.
Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010), American Economic Journal: Applied Economics. Source ↗
Sleep and social media
3×

Frequent checkers sleep worse.

Among 1,788 US adults aged 19–32, the most frequent social media checkers had about three times the odds of sleep disturbance versus the least frequent. Heavy daily users had about twice the odds.

In Stay CleanThe Night lock removes the late check-in.
Levenson et al. (2016), Preventive Medicine 85. An association, not proof of cause. Source ↗
Pacing social media
3 wks

Less use, less loneliness.

143 students limited Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes each per day. After three weeks they reported significantly lower loneliness and depression than the control group.

In Stay CleanThe 5-in-30 pacing is a gentler cap, not the same as this study's.
Hunt et al. (2018), Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology. Source ↗
Late eating
+8.3%

Late meals change how your body copes.

In a randomized crossover study of 845 people, blood glucose response was 8.3% higher after a dinner eaten one hour before bed than after one eaten four hours before.

In Stay CleanThe midnight order never gets placed.
Garaulet et al. (2022), Diabetes Care, via HealthDay. Source ↗
ⓘAn honest note. These studies are about the underlying ideas, not about Stay Clean, which has not been clinically tested. Results vary by person. Stay Clean is a self-help tool, not medical advice.

Install

On your phone in two minutes.

Stay Clean is for Android. It is not on iPhone, and not in the Play Store yet, so you install it directly from GitHub.

● Android 8.0 and up○ iPhone not supported
  1. Download the APKOpen the latest release on your phone and tap stay-clean-v1.3.apk.
  2. Allow the installIf Android asks, let your browser install unknown apps.
  3. Choose “Install anyway”Play Protect may warn because the app is new and uses Accessibility. Tap More details, then Install anyway.
  4. Finish the six setup rowsDo them top to bottom and do Guard last. If Guard is greyed out: Settings, Apps, Stay Clean, ⋮, Allow restricted settings.

Questions

Good to know.

No. There is no account, no analytics and no server. The only network request is a tiny time check, so the app can tell a real clock from a changed one. The code is open for anyone to read.

It is built as a speed bump for a weak moment, not a prison. Some settings are held shut at night, and it never locks you out of your own phone. A determined person can still remove it by day. That is the honest trade-off.

It uses the Accessibility permission to see which app is open, and it is installed outside the Play Store. Android and Play Protect are cautious about that combination. The source and a checksum are published so you can verify it.
